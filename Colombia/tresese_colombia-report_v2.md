# Colombian Tequila & Agave-Spirits Importers: Market-Entry Report for Tres Ese

*v2 — compiled 12 Sep 2026, fact-checked and corrected 14 Sep 2026. Same structure as the Germany report. Corrections vs v1 are listed at the end.*

## TL;DR
- **Colombia is the only Latin American market in the CRT's global top-10 tequila importers** — 6th worldwide on 2025 full-year CRT data (US, Spain, Germany, Canada, UK, Colombia, France, Japan, Italy, Australia). Tequila imports were ~5.5M litres worth US$44.5M in 2022 (latest importer-level breakdown), with the category growing 18–20%/year and up ~69% over five years while aguardiente lost 20%.
- **The market is concentrated: five importers move ~85% of tequila** — Dislicores (30%, Jose Cuervo/Proximo + Diageo on-trade), Pernod Ricard (26%, Olmeca/Altos), Global Wine & Spirits (17%, Patrón), Diageo (8%, Don Julio) and Vinpar (5%, value private labels). Jose Cuervo alone holds >50% of the segment. The realistic doors for Tres Ese are the **mid-size importers and specialist e-commerce players**, not the multinationals.
- **Zero import duty** (Mexico–Colombia TLC/G2, with a certificate of origin) and **no trademark conflict**. Two Colombian specifics dominate the cost model: **INVIMA sanitary registration** (held by the importer, ~15 working days once filed; BPM certificate no longer required since 14 Jan 2026) and the **impuesto al consumo** (per-degree specific tax + 25% ad valorem on retail price).
- **Tax outlook is clearer than in v1:** the emergency tax decree (Decreto 1474/2025) that would have doubled the specific rate and raised IVA to 19% was **declared unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court in April 2026 (Sentencia C-079/2026)** — it is dead. The live risk is the **Ley de Financiamiento filed in Congress on 23 Jul 2026**, which again proposes IVA on spirits 5% → 19% (consumo rates unchanged). Not approved as of Sep 2026.
- **Modelled shelf price for Blanco: ~COP 175,000 (~US$56) under today's rates; ~COP 197,000 (~US$64) if IVA goes to 19%.** Either way Tres Ese lands between Jose Cuervo Tradicional (COP 135k) and Don Julio Blanco (COP 238k), at or just below Altos Plata (COP 197k) — the premium 100%-agave tier where Colombian growth is.

## Market context (Colombia)
- **Global rank:** 6th tequila import market on 2025 full-year CRT data (Expansión, Jul 2026); 9th in the Jan 2025 monthly list. The only Latin American country in the top 10; Brazil's presence is "muy pequeña".
- **Size:** 5.5M litres of "tequila y similares" imported in 2022, valued at US$44.5M — 6.2% of all alcohol imports by volume but 17.5% by value (Centro Virtual de Negocios, Jun 2023). Mexico is Colombia's #1 source country for imported alcohol. No public 2024/2025 importer-level breakdown exists; ask the customs agent for DIAN data before presenting.
- **Growth:** +18–20% per year (RCN/Portafolio citing CRT, Oct 2025); tequila +69% over five years vs aguardiente −20% (Universidad EAN study of Dislicores). Consumers are "virando hacia productos premium, de origen certificado y alta calidad".
- **Premiumisation:** Patrón Cristalino, Don Ramón (ultra-premium line) and Casa Don Ramón mezcal all launched 2024 targeting the premium/gift season (Aug–Dec). 100% agave already accounts for ~73% of Mexico's total tequila exports (Jan 2025).
- **Mezcal:** Early but visible — Ojo de Tigre (Pernod), Casa Don Ramón, Siete Misterios and Contraluz in specialist retail; mezcal is a distinct category at Dislicores Store and Nóvili.
- **Cities:** Bogotá (largest on-trade + all importers HQ), Medellín (Dislicores home; Don Ramón chose Antioquia as its launch region via Carulla/Éxito), Cartagena (tourism/duty-free — slower post-pandemic per GWS), Cali, Barranquilla.
- **Seasonality:** August, September and November are the peak import months; year-end gifting drives premium launches.
- **Exchange rate:** TRM COP 3,072 per USD (in force 12–14 Sep 2026; range 3,072–3,116 the prior week); peso strong after a 7.5% rally in July 2026 — good for import pricing, but the fiscal gap (hence the Ley de Financiamiento) means volatility risk.

## Regulatory / import basics
- **Import duty:** 0% under the Mexico–Colombia TLC (G2, in force since 1995; 97% of tariff lines at 0%) with a certificate of origin. Pacific Alliance is the fallback. Tequila classifies under HS 2208.90. *Action: have the customs agent confirm the 2208.90 line in DIAN's arancel before quoting.*
- **INVIMA sanitary registration (registro sanitario):** Required for every imported spirit before customs release. Held by the **Colombian importer** (with the producer's authorisation); one registration can cover several expressions of the same brand with similar physico-chemical characteristics. INVIMA quotes **15 working days** from filing if no additional requirements are raised; plan 2–3 months end-to-end with document prep. The **Certificado de Venta Libre** (free-sale certificate) from Mexico is still mandatory. **Decreto 1083 de 2025 (in force 14 Jan 2026)** removed the mandatory BPM (GMP) certificate — a real simplification for a small distillery (BPM compliance itself is still expected). Registration is valid 10 years (5 years for the microempresa regime).
- **Labelling (Decreto 1686/2012 as amended):** Spanish label; imported spirits may use a **complementary sticker** carrying the mandatory legends, INVIMA registration number and importer name/address/city — no bottle redesign needed. Mandatory health warnings ("El exceso de alcohol es perjudicial para la salud. Ley 30 de 1986" / "Prohíbase el expendio de bebidas embriagantes a menores de edad. Ley 124 de 1994"). 700 ml is accepted (Don Julio, Altos, Dobel all sell in 700 ml).
- **Impuesto al consumo de licores (Ley 1816/2016)** — two components, paid by the importer at nationalisation:
  - Specific: **COP 360 per grado alcoholimétrico per 750 ml** (Certificación 003, MinHacienda, 9 Dec 2025) — for a 700 ml bottle at 40.4%: 40.4 × 360 × (700/750) = **COP 13,574**.
  - Ad valorem: **25%** of the DANE-certified retail price before taxes.
- **IVA:** 5% on spirits today.
- **Tax risk (Scenario B):** Decreto 1474/2025 (COP 750/degree, 30% ad valorem, IVA 19%) was provisionally suspended on 29 Jan 2026 (Autos 082 and 084/2026, expediente RE-388) and then **declared inexequible by Sentencia C-079/2026 (April 2026)**; the taxes collected 30 Dec 2025–28 Jan 2026 are being refunded. The remaining risk is the **Ley de Financiamiento (filed 23 Jul 2026)**: IVA on spirits 5% → 19%, consumo rates unchanged, beer excluded. Pending in Congress; DIAN estimates final prices +20–35% on strong spirits if passed. Model IVA at 19% as the downside.
- **Departmental monopoly:** Each departamento (Antioquia, Cundinamarca, Valle…) collects the consumption tax and can require a departmental registration/"estampilla" — the importer handles this; one reason a Bogotá-based importer with national coverage matters.

## Realism assessment
- **Dislicores — MEDIUM.** The dream partner for HORECA reach, but Jose Cuervo is the anchor and Don Julio is in the same house; a new organic blanco needs a slot they don't already fill (organic + additive-free is that slot). Expect them to ask for a bartender-education budget and a Medellín-first activation. Enter via the supplier portal, not marketing.
- **Vinpar — HIGH/MEDIUM.** Small (20 people), imports from second-tier Mexican distilleries under private labels — proves they will take an unknown brand and handle INVIMA. Risk: value positioning; keep Tres Ese out of the discount channel.
- **Nóvili — HIGH.** Importer + 6 stores + tasting programme = a self-contained launch platform across the main cities — and they list no tequila today (two mezcals only), so Tres Ese would be their tequila, not one of many. Two public emails to start with.
- **La Licorera — HIGH.** Bogotá e-commerce with national delivery and a track record of listing craft brands (Campo Azul, Ojo de Tigre). Fast to onboard, low budget, gives real sell-through data — but it is a retailer, so you still need an importer of record (Vinpar, Nóvili, Lumana or a customs-agent importer).
- **GWS — LOW-MEDIUM.** Premium-agency importer: will take a brand with a marketing plan and a clear price tier. Worth a conversation once Colombian sell-through exists.
- **Pernod, Diageo — LOW.** Own their agave brands.
- **Paneco, KYVA, Lumana — MEDIUM.** Listing/seeding partners rather than distribution muscle.

## Ranked recommendation
1. **Vinpar Comercializadora (importer of record) + La Licorera (launch retailer)** — the fastest, cheapest path to being legally on sale in Colombia with sell-through data in 90 days. Vinpar has the INVIMA/customs machinery for small Mexican brands; La Licorera gives national reach and real prices without a listing fee.
2. **Nóvili** — if they will import, a single partner with stores in 6 cities plus tastings; ideal for a premium, story-led brand. Two public emails make this the easiest first contact.
3. **Dislicores** — the year-2 target once there is Colombian proof; pitch a Medellín HORECA programme where Don Ramón has already shown premium tequila can be seeded via Antioquia.
4. **GWS** — keep warm; approach with a one-page marketing plan and the Colombian sell-through numbers.

**Outreach approach Colombian importers expect:** samples (2–3 SKUs), FOB price list in USD with an indicative COP shelf-price model (they will check it against the DANE PVP logic), certificate of origin ready, the Mexican free-sale certificate and product technical sheet for INVIMA, clarity on who pays INVIMA (usually the importer, with the brand covering translation/label sticker costs), a small activation budget (bartender kit, tastings — Colombia is a tasting culture), 12-month volume-triggered exclusivity, and a launch pegged to the Aug–Dec season. **Timing:** contacts made now can still target Expodrinks Caribe (12–15 Nov) and the Dec gift season for a pilot; a full launch fits Expovinos Aug–Sep 2027.

## Latin America expansion notes
- **Pacific Alliance (Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Chile)** gives the same 0%-duty logic for Peru and Chile; Panama and Ecuador require separate treatment.
- **Dislicores, GWS and Pernod/Diageo** operate Andean-region structures; a Colombian listing with any of them is a reference for Peru/Ecuador. Vinpar imports from Peruvian suppliers (Bodega San Isidro, Lima) — an existing Peru link.
- Brazil is the region's biggest untapped tequila market but has high taxes and low agave penetration; treat as phase 3.

## Caveats
- The importer share data (Dislicores 30%, Pernod 26%, GWS 17%, Diageo 8%, Vinpar 5%) is from the 2022 CVN report — the most recent public importer-level breakdown. Ask the customs agent for 2025 DIAN data before presenting.
- The Ley de Financiamiento is unresolved; present both IVA scenarios and flag that a vote could land in late 2026.
- Buyer-level emails are not public for any of the top-5; use the supplier portals/forms and LinkedIn. Public emails exist for Nóvili, La Licorera and KYVA.
- Competitor prices are from one Bogotá retailer on one date; prices vary by department because of the consumption tax and by retailer promotions.
- Freight, port and margin figures are estimates — get one forwarder quote (Guadalajara → Manzanillo → Cartagena, 1 pallet LCL) and one customs-agent quote (INVIMA + nationalisation) to firm up the model.
