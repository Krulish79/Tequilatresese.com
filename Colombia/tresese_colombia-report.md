# Colombian Tequila & Agave-Spirits Importers: Market-Entry Report for Tres Ese

*Compiled 12 Sep 2026 from direct web research. Same structure as the Germany report. Items marked "verify" should be re-checked the day before presenting.*

## TL;DR
- **Colombia is the only Latin American market in the CRT's global top-10 tequila importers** — the industry itself calls it out ("en América Latina el único que pinta en los primeros diez es Colombia"). Tequila imports were ~5.5M litres worth US$44.5M (2022, latest importer-level breakdown), with the category growing 18–20%/year and up ~69% over five years while aguardiente lost 20%.
- **The market is concentrated: five importers move ~85% of tequila** — Dislicores (30%, Jose Cuervo/Proximo + Diageo on-trade), Pernod Ricard (26%, Olmeca/Altos), Global Wine & Spirits (17%, Herradura/Patrón), Diageo (8%, Don Julio) and Vinpar (5%, value private labels). Jose Cuervo alone holds >50% of the tequila segment. The realistic doors for Tres Ese are the **mid-size premium importers and specialist e-commerce players**, not the multinationals.
- **Zero import duty** (G2 Mexico–Colombia FTA, total elimination; needs a G2 certificate of origin) and **no trademark conflict** — but two Colombian specifics dominate the cost model: **INVIMA sanitary registration** (held by the importer, ~15 working days once filed, BPM certificate no longer required since 14 Jan 2026) and the **impuesto al consumo** (a per-degree specific tax + ad valorem on retail price), which is currently in legal limbo between two rate scenarios.
- **Modelled shelf price for Blanco: ~COP 175,000 (~US$56) under today's tax rates, ~COP 250,000 (~US$81) if the suspended 2026 tax decree is upheld.** Either way Tres Ese lands between Altos Plata (COP 197k) and Don Julio Blanco (COP 238k) — the premium 100%-agave sipping tier, which is exactly where Colombian growth is.

## Market context (Colombia)
- **Global rank:** Consistently in the CRT top-10 tequila import markets (Jan 2025 monthly list: US, Germany, Spain, Canada, France, UK, China, Australia, Colombia, Japan; 2023–25 cumulative list per SADER: US, Japan, UK, Canada, Spain, Colombia, Italy, Australia, Germany, India). The only Latin American country in the list; Brazil's presence is described as "muy pequeña".
- **Size:** 5.5M litres of "tequila y similares" imported in 2022, valued at US$44.5M — 6.2% of all alcohol imports by volume but 17.5% by value (Centro Virtual de Negocios / Portafolio). Mexico is Colombia's #1 source country for imported alcohol.
- **Growth:** +18–20% per year (RCN, citing CRT); tequila segment +69% over five years vs aguardiente −20% (Universidad EAN study of Dislicores). Consumers "virando hacia productos premium, de origen certificado y alta calidad".
- **Premiumisation:** Patrón Cristalino, Don Ramón (ultra-premium line) and Casa Don Ramón mezcal all launched 2024 targeting the premium/gift season (Aug–Dec). 100% agave already accounts for ~73% of Mexico's total tequila exports (Jan 2025).
- **Mezcal:** Early but visible — Ojo de Tigre (Pernod) and Casa Don Ramón mezcal in retail; mezcal listed as a distinct category at Dislicores Store.
- **Cities:** Bogotá (largest on-trade + all importers HQ), Medellín (Dislicores home; Don Ramón chose Antioquia as its launch region via Carulla/Éxito), Cartagena (tourism/duty-free — slower post-pandemic per GWS), Cali.
- **Seasonality:** August, September and November are the peak import months; year-end gifting drives premium launches.
- **Exchange rate:** TRM ~COP 3,072–3,126 per USD (8–12 Sep 2026); peso strong after a 7.5% rally in July 2026 — good for import pricing, but the government's fiscal gap means volatility risk.

## Regulatory / import basics
- **Import duty:** 0% under the Mexico–Colombia FTA (G2/TLC, in force since 1995, "eliminación arancelaria total") with a G2 certificate of origin. Pacific Alliance is the fallback (92% of lines at 0%). Tequila classifies under HS 2208.90.
- **INVIMA sanitary registration (registro sanitario):** Required for every imported spirit before customs release. Held by the **Colombian importer** (with the producer's authorisation); one registration can cover several expressions of the same brand with similar physico-chemical characteristics. INVIMA quotes **15 working days** from filing if no additional requirements are raised; plan 2–3 months end-to-end with document prep (Certificado de Venta Libre / free-sale certificate from Mexico still mandatory). **Decreto 1083 de 2025 (in force 14 Jan 2026)** removed the mandatory BPM (GMP) certification requirement — a significant simplification for a small distillery. Registration validity is 10 years (5 years + one renewal for the microempresario regime).
- **Labelling (Decreto 1686/2012 as amended):** Spanish label; imported spirits may use a **complementary sticker** carrying the mandatory legends, INVIMA registration number and importer name/address/city — no bottle redesign needed. Mandatory health warnings ("El exceso de alcohol es perjudicial para la salud. Ley 30 de 1986" / "Prohíbase el expendio de bebidas embriagantes a menores de edad. Ley 124 de 1994"). 700 ml is accepted (Don Julio, Altos, Dobel all sell in 700 ml).
- **Impuesto al consumo de licores (Ley 1816/2016)** — two components, paid by the importer at nationalisation:
  - Specific: **COP 360 per grado alcoholimétrico per 750 ml** (Certificación 003, MinHacienda, Dec 2025) — for a 700 ml bottle at 40.4%: 40.4 × 360 × (700/750) = **COP 13,574**.
  - Ad valorem: **25%** of the DANE-certified retail price before taxes.
  - **Scenario B (suspended):** Decreto 1474 of 29 Dec 2025 raised the specific rate to **COP 750/degree (+119%)**, ad valorem to **30%**, and IVA on spirits from **5% to 19%**. The Constitutional Court **provisionally suspended** the decree on 29 Jan 2026 (expediente RE-387); if it is ultimately upheld the higher rates could apply retroactively from 1 Jan 2026. Accountants are advising importers to provision the difference. **Model both scenarios.**
- **IVA:** 5% on spirits today (Scenario A); 19% if Scenario B is upheld.
- **Departmental monopoly:** Each departamento (Antioquia, Cundinamarca, Valle…) collects the consumption tax and can require a departmental registration/"estampilla" — the importer handles this; it is one reason a Bogotá-based importer with national coverage matters.

## Realism assessment
- **Dislicores — MEDIUM.** The dream partner for HORECA reach, but Jose Cuervo is the anchor and Don Julio is in the same house; a new organic blanco needs a slot they don't already fill (organic + additive-free is that slot). Expect them to ask for a bartender-education budget and a Medellín-first activation.
- **Vinpar — HIGH/MEDIUM.** Small (20 people), imports from second-tier Mexican distilleries under private labels — proves they will take an unknown brand and handle INVIMA. Risk: value positioning; keep Tres Ese out of the discount channel.
- **La Licorera — HIGH.** Bogotá e-commerce with national delivery and a track record of listing craft brands (Campo Azul, Ojo de Tigre). Fast to onboard, low budget, gives real sell-through data — but it is a retailer, so you still need an importer of record (Vinpar, Nóvili or a customs-agent importer).
- **Nóvili — HIGH.** Importer + 7 stores + tasting programme = a self-contained launch platform across the main cities — and they carry no tequila today (only Siete Misterios mezcal), so Tres Ese would be their tequila, not one of many.
- **Dicermex / GWS — MEDIUM / LOW-MEDIUM.** Premium-agency importers: they will take a brand with a marketing plan and a clear price tier. Worth a conversation once Colombian sell-through exists.
- **Pernod, Diageo — LOW.** Own their agave brands.
- **Paneco, KYVA, Lumana — MEDIUM.** Listing/seeding partners rather than distribution muscle.

## Ranked recommendation
1. **Vinpar Comercializadora (importer of record) + La Licorera (launch retailer)** — the fastest, cheapest path to being legally on sale in Colombia with sell-through data in 90 days. Vinpar has the INVIMA/customs machinery for small Mexican brands; La Licorera gives national reach and real prices without a listing fee.
2. **Nóvili** — if they will import, a single partner with stores in 7 cities plus tastings; ideal for a premium, story-led brand.
3. **Dislicores** — the year-2 target once there is Colombian proof; pitch a Medellín HORECA programme where Don Ramón has already shown premium tequila can be seeded via Antioquia.
4. **Dicermex / GWS** — keep warm; approach with a one-page marketing plan and the Colombian sell-through numbers.

**Outreach approach Colombian importers expect:** samples (2–3 SKUs), FOB price list in USD with an indicative COP shelf-price model (they will check it against the DANE PVP logic), G2 certificate of origin ready, the Mexican free-sale certificate and product technical sheet for INVIMA, clarity on who pays INVIMA (usually the importer, with the brand covering translation/label sticker costs), a small activation budget (bartender kit, tastings — Colombia is a tasting culture), 12-month volume-triggered exclusivity, and a launch pegged to the Aug–Dec season.

## Latin America expansion notes
- **Pacific Alliance (Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Chile)** gives the same 0%-duty logic for Peru and Chile; Panama and Ecuador require separate treatment.
- **Dislicores, GWS and Pernod/Diageo** operate Andean-region structures; a Colombian listing with any of them is a reference for Peru/Ecuador. Vinpar imports from Peruvian suppliers (Bodega San Isidro, Lima) — an existing Peru link.
- Brazil is the region's biggest untapped tequila market but has high taxes and low agave penetration; treat as phase 3.

## Caveats
- The importer share data (Dislicores 30%, Pernod 26%, GWS 17%, Diageo 8%, Vinpar 5%) is from the 2022 CVN report — the most recent public importer-level breakdown found. Ask the customs agent for 2025 DIAN data before presenting.
- The impuesto al consumo is genuinely unresolved (Constitutional Court expediente RE-387). Present both scenarios and flag that a ruling could land any time in late 2026.
- Buyer-level emails are not public for any of the top-5; use the general contacts and LinkedIn. Only La Licorera has a public contact email in this list.
- Competitor prices are from one Bogotá retailer on one date; prices vary by department because of the consumption tax and by retailer promotions.
- Freight, port and margin figures are estimates — get one forwarder quote (Guadalajara → Manzanillo → Cartagena, 1 pallet LCL) and one customs-agent quote (INVIMA + nationalisation) to firm up the model.
